## 1. Purpose

This Index exists so that anyone — Product Owner, engineer, or a fresh session with no prior context — can answer three questions without archaeology: which document is the current source of truth for a given module, what its real approval status is, and how it relates to every other specification. Without an index, status has to be inferred from each document's own header, which this project has already found to go stale (`docs/ORDERS_DATABASE.md`'s header once cited a "Draft" companion doc as "approved and locked"; `docs/INVENTORY_SPEC.md`'s own header still says "Draft... awaiting further review" despite later work having proceeded against it).

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** every document in `docs/` that states or governs a CRM business rule — Foundation Documents, Business Specifications, Planned Specifications, and Legacy Documents (§4–§7) — for the **CRM Operating System** only. **One explicit exception (Product Owner Decision, 2026-07-31):** `database/foundation/17_DATABASE_FOUNDATION.md` lives outside `docs/` but is included in §4 as the official Database Foundation document — see that section for why.

**Out of scope, explicitly:** the Jade Intelligence **Platform** document set (`KNOWLEDGE_VAULT_SPEC.md`, `CANONICAL_DATA_MODEL.md`, `TAXONOMY_AND_ONTOLOGY.md`, `COLLECTOR_FRAMEWORK.md`, `MASTER_TECHNICAL_SPECIFICATION.md`, and similar) — that is a deliberately separate, non-CRM project (implementation lives in a different repository entirely) and was explicitly scoped to not touch or be governed by CRM module specs when it was opened. Listing it here would misrepresent it as a CRM module.

## 3. Specification Hierarchy

```
Planned Specifications (§6)
   — reserved module slots, zero content today, no authority until drafted
        │
        ▼  (drafting begins)
Foundation Documents (§4)
   — project-wide rules every Business Specification must not contradict
        │
        ▼
Business Specifications (§5)
   — one per module, the current source of truth for that module's business rules
        │
        ▼  (superseded)
Legacy Documents (§7)
   — superseded, reference-only; never authoritative once superseded
```

A Business Specification may **reference** a Foundation Document (e.g. `docs/03_ORDER_SPEC.md` referencing `docs/EVENT_CATALOG.md` for event naming) but must never restate or contradict it — this is the same Foundation Rule already established inside `docs/EVENT_CATALOG.md` itself, generalized here to the whole hierarchy, not just events.

## 8. Specification Ownership

**Business Rule:** Every Business Specification has exactly one owning module and, per this project's standing role definition, exactly one authoring authority — the Product Owner decides business rules; the Engineering Lead drafts, proposes, and applies exactly what's decided, never originating a rule unprompted. This mirrors `docs/EVENT_CATALOG.md` §4's "one Publisher per event" rule, generalized: one Product Owner decision authority per specification, not per event.

A module's own database-design document (e.g. `docs/ORDERS_DATABASE.md`) is owned by the same authority as its business spec and must not contradict it — where a conflict has occurred (Product Status vocabulary, Snapshot scope), the business specification's later, LOCKED decision governs, and the database document is revised to match, not the reverse.

## 9. Specification Lifecycle

**Business Rule, per Product Owner Decision 1 (2026-07-30):** Every Business Specification moves through five defined stages, in order, and is never labeled with more than one at a time:

`PLANNED` → `DRAFT` → `REVISION` → `LOCKED` → `LEGACY`

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PLANNED** | Reserved module slot. File may exist but has zero content, or does not exist yet. No authority — nothing to consult (§6). |
| **DRAFT** | First pass written. No Product Owner decision has been applied yet. |
| **REVISION** *(numbered, Revision 1, 2, 3…)* | At least one Product Owner decision cycle has been applied. Open Questions may still remain. A specification that has already had a Revision applied is **never** still labeled "Draft" — this is the exact mismatch flagged for Inventory, Reports, Jade Intelligence, and Market Intelligence in `docs/BUSINESS_SPEC_STATUS_ALIGNMENT.md`. |
| **LOCKED** | No open Product Owner Questions remain. Read-only — further change requires a new Revision, never a silent edit (per `[[project_rules_v1]]`'s Specification rule). Customer, Product, and Order (§5) have all reached this stage. |
| **LEGACY** | Superseded by a later document. Retained for reference only, never authoritative once superseded (§7). |

## 10. Governance

**Foundation Rule:** This Index is the authoritative map of *which document governs what and its current status* — it is not itself an authority over any business rule's *content*. If this Index and a Business Specification ever disagree about status, the specification's own header governs its own status; if two Business Specifications disagree about a rule that touches both (e.g. Order Spec and Product Spec both describing Product Status), the more specific, most-recently-LOCKED document governs, and the other is revised to match — the same precedent already set between `docs/02_PRODUCT_SPEC.md` and `docs/ORDERS_DATABASE.md`.

**Governance Rule:** No specification is added to §5 (Business Specifications), added to or removed from §6 (Planned Specifications), or moved between categories in this Index without a Product Owner decision — the same discipline `docs/EVENT_CATALOG.md` §13 applies to Business Events, applied here to specifications themselves.

## 11. Revision Rules

**Business Rule:** This Index must be updated whenever a specification changes stage (§9) or a new one is created — otherwise it becomes exactly the kind of stale artifact §5 already had to flag for four existing specs. Updating this Index is a lightweight table edit, not a full Revision-approval cycle, **except** when the change itself reflects a real Product Owner decision (e.g. declaring a spec LOCKED or LEGACY) — that decision is what's approved; this Index just records it.
